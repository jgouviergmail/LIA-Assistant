# Relations (personal CRM)

## What is the Relations page?
Open **Relations** in the navigation bar: one card per person, aggregating what LIA already tracks — **open commitments**, **phone calls**, **memories** that mention them and **relayed messages**.

Opening a card shows the 360° sheet, whose first section is where you launch a 360° recap in the chat. It is a lens over existing data: nothing new is collected.

## How do favorites work?
Tap the **star** on any card (or in the 360° sheet) — no need to open anything. Favorites keep their own band at the top of the page, and a starred person **never disappears**, even when there has been no interaction for a long while.

Tap the star again to unstar; with many relations, use the name filter that appears above the list.

## What is the small LIA badge on some cards?
It marks relations who are **also connected users** of your LIA instance (see the Peer Connections section). The badge is read-only and only appears when the connections feature is enabled — a bridge between your personal CRM and assistant-to-assistant connections.

## Why does a card say the match is best-effort?
People are grouped by **name**, with accents and case forgiven. When the spellings differ across sources, or when a memory merely mentions a common first name, LIA says the match is **best-effort** rather than pretending to be sure — honesty over false precision.

## What does a relationship card show?
Beyond what LIA tracks itself, the card shows what your connected accounts know about that person: their **address-book entry** (nickname, job title, birthday, postal addresses, relations, links…), the **mail exchanged** and the **meetings shared**.

Those three are found by **email address**, resolved from the contact card — with no address on file, LIA says so instead of pretending it found nothing. They state the **window** they looked at rather than a total a provider page cannot prove, and each carries a refresh control.

## How do I choose what LIA reads for a 360° recap?
The first section of the card, **360° recap**, holds the checkboxes: which sources to use (contact card, commitments, memories, calls, relayed messages, mail, meetings), the **direction** of exchanges (received, sent), the **role** in meetings (attendee, organizer) and how many items each section may contribute.

Your choice is **saved before the chat opens**, so LIA applies it instead of guessing it from your sentence — and it pre-fills next time. Clearing everything is not the same as taking everything: an unticked source is simply not read.

## Why did a connected person have no mail and no meetings?
Exchanges are found by **address**, not by name: someone you never saved in your address book had none, so there was nothing to match. If that person is a **connected user** and has turned on address sharing (Connections setting), LIA now uses it to find, in **your** mail and **your** calendar, the exchanges and meetings they appear in.

Without that sharing nothing changes: their setting stays the only thing that decides. And if no meeting involves them, the section stays **empty** — LIA does not fill the silence with an event of your calendar they neither organise nor attend.
