# Tools — your services, by hand or with LIA's help

## What is the « Tools » section?

**Tools**, in the header, opens your connected services directly so you can consult them and act on them **yourself**, as you would in your provider's own app. What you do by hand involves no model: it costs no token and nothing in euros, answers as fast as your provider does, and does exactly what you click. A row of tools above the open one switches between them in one tap — on a phone it scrolls sideways — and the section opens on the first tool that is ready. On a large screen, drag the line between two panes to widen one, or double-click it to give the pane its width back; this device remembers it. **Refresh** reads a tool again, and coming back to the page after a while does it on its own. The open tool's pill says whose service it is, and on a computer **?** lists its keyboard shortcuts, which you can switch off there. **Ask LIA**, on a message, an event, a task, a contact or a file, opens the chat on it, ready for your words: nothing leaves until you send. **Ctrl+K**, or the magnifier beside the row of tools, searches your mail, calendars, contacts and files at once: each tool shows its first results, and says if it could not answer.

## Why is a tool listed under « To connect »?

A tool works once its service is connected in **Settings › Connectors**: mail, calendar, contacts and tasks use the provider active for that category (Google, Microsoft or Apple — Apple has no tasks), files use the drive you connected, Google Drive or OneDrive, and lights your Hue bridge. Until then the tool waits under **To connect**, with a link to the connectors. A tool your administrator switched off is not shown at all.

## Does LIA see or remember what I do in Tools?

What you do by hand is your act, not LIA’s: it adds no entry to LIA’s registers, memory, habits, journal or AI usage costs. This does not switch off features you enabled separately: the chat, routines, knowledge synchronisation and opted-in monitoring retain their own access and permissions. What you ask of LIA from these screens is LIA’s act: see “What can LIA do from the Tools?”.

## What can I do with my mail and my calendar?

**Mail**: browse your folders (and labels with Gmail), search, read a message as text or in its original layout — shown in an isolated frame where nothing can run, its links opening in a new tab and its remote images waiting for your click, which tells the sender you opened it — mark read or unread, trash, label (Gmail), write, reply, forward with your own files attached, keep drafts, replies and forwards included, and with Gmail or Outlook set your vacation responder and filters.

**Calendar**: Agenda, Day, Week or Month, each calendar shown or hidden, events created, changed and deleted — all-day ones and video calls where your provider supports them. In a repeating series, a change reaches that occurrence alone unless you choose the whole series; with iCloud it always reaches the series, and the screen says so. In a message's recipients and an event's guests, typing a name suggests your contacts' addresses. The addresses in a message's text and in your notes open with a click, a filter can be deleted here, and a new event goes to the default calendar of your connector's preferences. A message's row has its own actions, Shift ticks a whole range, and on a computer the next message opens after one you trash. On a large screen the mailbox opens on your newest message, which stays unread until you open it yourself. The message you write stays beside the mail, which you can go on reading, and **Send** waits a few seconds so you can take it back. A message can become a task or an event, and its sender a contact. In the **Day** and **Week** views a line shows the time, a click on an empty slot starts an event there, and on a computer an event moves by dragging it and lengthens by its lower edge. If it has guests, the editor opens on the new time and nothing leaves until you save. **Agenda** lists the next thirty days, a video call opens from the event itself, an event can be duplicated, and on a phone **Month** shows the days with their dots, the picked day's events below. The images a message carries inside itself show on their own; only those it fetches from elsewhere wait for your click. An attachment opens in a window of the page, and a message's text prints from its menu. When a mailing list says how to leave it, **Unsubscribe** stands under the sender: one click after a question where the list promises it, else its own page or a message to send.

**Sorting your mail**: archive, move to a folder (from the menu, or by dragging a row onto the folder pane on a computer), star, report as spam or not, restore from the trash; **Undo** on the toast brings it back. Outlook and iCloud also delete for good and empty the trash, after a confirmation, and manage your folders; Gmail deletes what stays in its trash on its own after 30 days. The inbox shows its unread count on the Mail pill, and each folder its own.

**Your calendar**: answer an invitation from the event (accept, maybe, decline: the organiser is told), search your events, add a call to an event that has none, set a reminder, show the time as free, keep an event private, choose its time zone, send it to another calendar (on Outlook and iCloud, an event with guests or of a series stays where it is), create, rename and delete your own calendars, and see your dated tasks on their day.

**Writing**: attach a file from your device with the button or by dropping it on the message, on a reply and a forward too. A reply shows whom it reaches, and you can change its recipients and copies. A new message saves itself when you stop writing; a reply or a forward is kept with **Draft**, in its conversation, to finish from your drafts. A draft keeps its files. **Formatting** adds bold, italic, lists and links; what you paste arrives as plain words. Your **signature**, in the mailbox settings, closes your messages and those LIA prepares for you, where you read it on the card. With Gmail, choose the address a message leaves from when your mailbox has several. A message shows its **conversation** above its text, and the list can group each conversation into one line. An event can repeat, and a change or a deletion can reach the whole series. An Outlook connection made before these settings asks to be reconnected once for them.

## And my tasks, contacts, files and lights?

**Tasks**: your lists and their tasks, ticked done (undone at once if your provider refuses), created, edited, deleted.

**Contacts**: sorted by last name then first name, each shown as first name then last name; browse, search, filter by group, find the « other contacts » met by mail (Google); every address, phone number and postal address is kept and edited one by one, and **See the relationship** opens the Relations page when the names match exactly.

**Files**, on Google Drive or OneDrive: your drive and its folders, a search over all of it, a file's preview and details, the trash with **Undo**. Create a folder, send files with the button or by dropping them on the list, rename, move, copy, share with someone or by a link, send a file by mail, and keep a mail's attachment on the drive. A Google document downloads as PDF or Word, a sheet as Excel, a presentation as PDF or PowerPoint. Google Drive adds its views (recent, starred, shared with you, shared drives), stars, and its trash, restored or emptied for good by hand after a question; OneDrive lists none of them, and SharePoint libraries are not listed. In the chat, LIA organises your files the same way and shows a card before it shares one.

**Lights**: rooms, lamps and scenes, with only the controls each lamp supports. Rooms and lamps come in alphabetical order, and a lamp's temperature and colour wait in a section you open. The tasks screen opens on **All tasks**; the default list of your connector's preferences is only where a new task goes when you name no list in the chat. An « other contact » opens read-only, and a scene no room or zone holds stays within reach under them. Tasks save as you leave a field, and today, tomorrow or next week are one tap away. A contact's addresses and numbers copy in one tap, a postal address opens on a map, and an e-mail address opens a new message. Files show what they are, sort by name, date or size, go to the trash several at a time, and their link copies. **All off** switches off everything lit, and on a phone a room's scenes are on its card. Once nothing is lit, the button becomes **All on**. **Today**, **Overdue** and **Coming up** gather the tasks of all your lists, **All** shows the three together, and **All tasks**, at the head of your lists, holds every task still to do, dated or not; each one names its list in a badge and opens beside the view, without leaving it; you can rename a list, delete its completed tasks, select several tasks at once and send a task to another list. With Microsoft To Do a task also keeps steps, an importance, a reminder and a repetition. A contact also holds a job, a website, a birthday and a photo; groups work with the three providers, and a vCard file exports or imports your address book. Zones sit after the rooms. A room or a zone takes a temperature or a colour as a whole, a lamp your bridge cannot reach says so, and a scene whose palette can play has a button that sets it in motion.

## What if I click twice, or the connection drops while sending?

The screen guards repeated submissions to reduce duplicate sends and creates. When the earlier submission is still recorded, a second click is told “already submitted” instead of sending again. If the answer was lost, the outcome is shown as unknown: check Sent or the relevant service before submitting again. With the moment to take a message back, **Undo** stops it before it leaves; leaving the tool meanwhile sends it, since you pressed Send.

## Can I read a file or an attachment without saving it, and change a text?

Yes. A file’s detail and a message’s attachments open in the page, on every device: a PDF whole, page after page, a Word file laid out as its author wrote it, a Markdown formatted, a spreadsheet or a CSV as a table, a sheet at a time, an image, a text. With Google Drive a Google document, and with OneDrive a Word file or a slide deck, arrive as a PDF the provider renders. A slide deck attached to a mail or kept on Google Drive reads as text. A long text says it stops before its end, and **Enlarge** gives the document a window. If a reading fails, **Try again** starts it over. A plain text file can be changed with **Edit the text**: if it changed elsewhere meanwhile, nothing is written and your words stay. An Office file or a Google document is changed where it lives, behind **Open**. LIA reads, adds to and rewrites a text file too, after a card showing the whole new text.

## What does a contact’s detail show of our exchanges?

Your latest exchanges with them: the five latest messages they sent you, the five latest you sent them, the five latest meetings they organised with you invited, and the five latest you organised with them invited. A line opens in its tool. The calendar is read back over the last two years, in your default calendar, and the screen says so. A meeting someone else organised answers neither list, and one that names no organiser stands in a list of its own. If your mailbox or your calendar could not be read, it says so rather than showing nothing.

With their numbers come the five latest calls LIA placed to them, each saying when, how it went and what it was for, and the five latest texts they sent you: a contact with numbers alone has exchanges too. A call opens in your calls list, a text on the SMS page. These lists appear once LIA has calls or texts to show, and never show the number itself.

## What can LIA do from the Tools?

The yellow stars mark where LIA can help. The ones that end every bar open a list, **Ask LIA** last. There LIA offers what it can do on what you are looking at: **sum up** or **translate** a message, sum up a conversation, **propose a reply**, make an event or a task of it, **read it aloud**, sum up a file or an attachment, describe an image, complete a contact from their messages and, with Microsoft To Do, break a task into steps. Where you write, the same icon writes from an instruction, rephrases, translates, proposes a subject and lets you **dictate**. One sentence is enough to create an event or a task, search your mail or fill a filter, and a photo of a business card fills a new contact. So does a sentence, typed or said: a value is kept only if the sentence says it, a number by its digits. On a contact, **Call** asks LIA to phone one of their numbers once your telephony is connected: the chat opens on the words, and LIA asks before it dials. **Write to … with LIA** opens a new message ready for your instruction, and their notes have a writing helper too. Select words in a message's text or in a file shown in its panel: a bubble beside them explains, translates, simplifies or answers your question about them, without leaving what you read, and in a message it also makes a task or an event of them. A message shown in its original layout turns to its text with **Act on a passage**. On an event, **Write to the guests with LIA** opens a message to each of them; on a file, **Send by mail with LIA** attaches it to a message written from your instruction; on a task, **Block a slot in the calendar** opens a new event on its title and its day, which you save or not. In the list of messages, **Sort** puts the important ones first, groups by kind or keeps only the page's priority messages. A task or an event LIA reads in a message opens in its full form with everything the message says: who asks and what, the day, the place or the video link, the list it belongs to and, when the message sets up a meeting with you, its sender as a guest: saving sends them the invitation. A task found in a summary is read again for that action alone, and an instruction can be said rather than typed.

Nothing leaves without your click and nothing is written at your provider: LIA proposes, you read, you finish by hand. Each assist uses a model or a voice, only when you ask: what it costs shows beside the answer and counts in your usage, and what it read of your services stands in your **Consultations**, like any read of LIA’s. A value proposed for a contact comes with the message that says it, and is only proposed if that message really does. What you do by hand stays free of any model and any cost.

The same lists also offer **Remind me**, **Ticket for LIA** (a ticket on the board), **Keep in a space** and, on a message, **Tell me when they reply**. They end on a ready question for the chat, such as **Prepare this meeting** or **Take stock**: you read it, and nothing leaves until you send it. On an event, **See the minutes** opens the recorded meeting, and **Record this meeting** appears as it draws near. Your administrator can switch these assists off: the Tools then stay entirely manual.

## Which model answers, and who can switch these assists off?

What you write and what a sentence asks go through the **Tool Assist** line of the LLM configuration, a message’s summary and sorting through **Email Digest**, writing a message through **Emails Agent**, an image or a scan through **Vision Analysis**: your administrator sets them. An answer already computed comes back at no cost.

An administrator switches the assists off in two places:
• **Settings › Administration › Platform capabilities** › **LIA in the Tools**, at once, with no redeployment
• the server configuration (TOOL_ASSISTS_ENABLED=false)

Once off, the lines served by LIA leave, **Keep in a space** and reading aloud included: what you do by hand, your reminders and your tickets stay.

## Why can’t I do something my provider’s own app does?

Each screen offers only what your provider’s connection lets LIA do: a gesture one provider lacks is not offered rather than offered and refused. A page cut at a limit always says so.

## How do the text menus work throughout LIA?

Select readable text anywhere in LIA, including chat, for **Read, Question, Explain, Summarize, Simplify, Translate, Internet, Task, Event, Reminder, Ticket, Remember or LIA**. Answers and audio stay in a compact bubble. Creation actions open the corresponding prefilled form for you to adjust and save; a reminder without a clear time needs completing. LIA opens chat with an unsent draft. While editing, select text for **Translate, Correct, Format, Shorten, More formal or Warmer**. Translation lets you choose a suggested language or enter another. Replacement always needs your click. Empty fields and collapsed selections have no contextual menu; dictation uses its separate controls. Moving focus to another field closes the editing menu. Internet names its actual search service and source links; unavailable capabilities cannot run. AI and voice use the usual usage limits and show their cost; a connected search service may bill your own key.
