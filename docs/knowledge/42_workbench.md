# Tools — your services, by hand

## What is the « Tools » section?

**Tools** (in the header) opens your connected services directly — your mail, calendar, tasks, contacts, files and Hue lights — so you can consult them and act on them **yourself**, the way you would in the provider's own application. No model is involved: it costs no token and nothing in euros, it answers as fast as your provider does, and it does exactly what you click.

A row of the six tools sits above the open one and switches between them in one tap — on a phone it scrolls sideways — and the section opens on the first tool that is ready. On a large screen, drag the line between two panes to widen one, or double-click it to give the pane its width back; this device remembers it. An item you open (a message, an event, a contact, a file) is part of the page's address, so the browser's back button closes it.

**Refresh**, at the end of the row of tools, reads a tool again; coming back to the page after a while does it on its own, without taking back the pages you went through. The open tool's pill says whose service it is.

On a computer, **?** lists the open tool's keyboard shortcuts (a single key, while you are not typing), and the same window switches them off on this device. **Ask LIA**, on a message, an event, a task, a contact or a file, opens the chat on it, ready for your words: nothing leaves until you send.

## Which tools appear, and why is one « To connect »?

A tool appears once its service is connected in **Settings › Connectors**: mail, calendar, contacts and tasks use the provider that is active for that category (Google, Microsoft or Apple — Apple has no tasks), files use the drive you connected (Google Drive or OneDrive, one at a time), and lights use your Hue bridge. A tool whose service is not connected yet is listed under **To connect**, with a link to the connectors. A tool your administrator switched off is not shown at all.

## Does LIA see or remember what I do in Tools?

What you do by hand is your act, not LIA's: it adds no entry to LIA's registers,
memory, habits, journal or AI usage costs. This does not switch off features you
enabled separately: the chat, routines, knowledge synchronisation and opted-in
monitoring retain their own access and permissions.

## What can I do with my mail?

Browse your folders (and your labels with Gmail), search, read a message as text or in its **original layout** — shown in an isolated frame where nothing can run, its links opening in a new tab and its remote images waiting for your click: loading them tells the sender you opened the message, and your IP address — mark messages read or unread, move them to the trash, apply labels (Gmail), write, reply, reply to all and forward (with your own files attached), keep and send drafts, and, with Gmail or Outlook, set your vacation responder and create and delete your filters, which Outlook calls rules (the mail a filter already sorted stays where it is). Your own folders stand under their parent, Outlook's included. The addresses in a message's text open with a click, a long one drawn by its site's name; only web, mail and phone addresses are followed. An attachment opens from its card in a window of the page, its download beside it; a file the page cannot read is a download. Typing a name in the recipients suggests your contacts' addresses.

The images a message carries inside itself show on their own: they ask nobody for anything. Only those it fetches from elsewhere wait for your click. An attached file opens in a window of the page (see *Reading and changing a file*), and a message's text prints from its menu. In a dark theme a message's original layout turns dark, its pictures keeping their colours, a very bright colour a little paler: one click gives the sender's colours back. When a mailing list says how to leave it, **Unsubscribe** stands under the sender: one click after a question where the list promises it, else its own page or a message for you to send. LIA's server makes that one click, so your address does not reach the sender's page.

A message's row has its own actions, and Shift ticks a whole range. On a computer, the next message opens after one you trash. The message you write stays in a corner of the screen, so the mail stays readable and usable behind it, and on a phone a reply keeps the message it answers in sight. **Send** waits a few seconds so you can take the message back with **Undo**; leaving the tool in that moment sends it, since you pressed Send, and if it then fails you are told and can copy its text. A message can become a task or an event pointing back at it, and its sender a contact.

Sort a message from its row, from the reader or on a selection: **archive** it, **move** it to a folder (with the menu, or on a computer by dragging the row onto the folder pane), **star** it, report it as **spam** or say it is not one, **restore** it from the trash. **Undo**, on the message that says it is done, brings it back to where it was; a message you sorted from a view that mixes folders (the starred, the sent, Gmail's all mail) has no place to come back to, and the undo is not offered. With Gmail, an archived message is found in **All mail**.

Outlook and iCloud also **delete for good** a message of the trash and **empty the trash**, each after a confirmation that says it cannot be undone. Gmail's connection does not allow it: Gmail deletes what stays in its trash on its own after 30 days. Only you can delete for good: the assistant can sort your mail, but never that. On Outlook and iCloud, **Manage folders** creates, renames and deletes your own folders; a folder that still holds messages or folders is never deleted, so that deleting one cannot lose anything.

The Mail pill says what your inbox leaves unread, on every tool's page, and each folder of the pane its own; the list reads itself again when something arrives. A paperclip on a row says the message carries files: exact on Outlook; on Gmail and iCloud it is read from how the message is built, which holds for what most mailers send.

When you write, attach a file from your device with the button or by dropping it on the message, on a reply and a forward too, or choose one of your files or one of your drive. A reply shows whom it reaches, and you can change its recipients and its copies. A draft keeps its files, and the composer saves it by itself when you stop writing, says so, and leaves it in your drafts if you close. **Formatting** adds bold, italic, underline, lists and links; what you paste arrives as plain words, and a draft you open again is edited as text. Your **signature**, in the mailbox settings on every provider, closes your messages and the ones LIA prepares for you, where you read it on the card and can change it. With Gmail you choose the address a message leaves from when your mailbox has several.

A message that belongs to a conversation says so above its text: open that line to see who wrote what and when, and click a message to read it. The button at the top of the list **groups by conversation**: each one is then a single line under its newest message, and what you do to that line reaches its messages loaded in the list, a star going to the newest. Ask LIA for the whole exchange around a message and it reads the conversation in one go, its latest messages when there are many.

## What can I do with my calendar?

See your calendars in **Agenda**, **Day**, **Week** or **Month**, show or hide each calendar, create, change and delete events — all-day ones included, with a video call where your provider offers one, and telling the guests where your provider lets you choose. In a repeating series, a change or a deletion reaches that occurrence alone unless you choose the whole series; with iCloud it always reaches the series, and the screen says so. Times are shown in your account's time zone. Typing a name in the guests suggests your contacts' addresses, as for a message's recipients. A new event goes to the default calendar of your connector's preferences, else to your own. The addresses in an event's place and notes open with a click.

A line shows the time in **Day** and **Week**. A click on an empty slot starts an event there, on the hour or the half hour, and a click in the all-day row starts an all-day one. On a computer, drag an event to another time or day, or stretch it by its lower edge: a move is sent at once and can be undone from the message that follows, but **an event with guests opens its editor on the new time, and nothing leaves until you save**, so nobody is told by a slip of the mouse. An event across midnight, an all-day one and a series your provider cannot change one occurrence of do not drag; the editor changes them. **Agenda** lists the next thirty days from the day shown. A video call opens from the event itself, a series wears its mark, and an event can be duplicated, also from a calendar you cannot write to. On a phone, **Month** shows the days with a dot per calendar, the picked day's events below.

**Invitations.** An event you are invited to asks for your answer, the current one stated: **Accept**, **Maybe** or **Decline**, and the organiser is told. On Outlook, a declined event leaves your calendar. LIA can answer for you too: it shows a card first, and nothing leaves before you confirm it.

**Search.** The box above the view looks for your words in the calendars you show, a year on each side of today, and the answer takes the view's place. A calendar that could not be read is said, and so is a list cut at its bound.

**What an event says beyond its time.** In the editor: a **reminder**, shown as **free** rather than busy, kept **private**, a **call** added to an event that has none (Google Meet or Teams), and its **time zone**: an event opens on the clock it was set on, and the detail says its time there when it is not your account's. LIA sets these too, on the card it asks you to confirm.

**Another calendar.** **Move to** sends an event to another calendar you can write in. Google moves it. Outlook and iCloud have no such move: the event is copied and the original removed, so an event with guests or of a series stays where it is, and on Outlook one with attachments too; an Outlook event also loses its own time zone, its time staying right. An invitation is the organiser's and moves nowhere. If the original could not be removed, the page says the event now stands in both.

**Your calendars.** **Manage calendars** (the small button above the list, or the Calendars menu on a phone) creates, renames and deletes your own calendars; your main calendar and those shared with you stay as they are. Deleting a calendar deletes its events, for good, after a confirmation.

**Your tasks in the calendar.** Your open tasks with a date appear on their day, as a link into **Tasks**, once the tasks are connected. Hide them like a calendar, on this device. A list that could not be read, or one too long to read whole, is said above the view.

## And my tasks, contacts, files and lights?

- **Tasks**: your lists and their tasks; tick a task done (it is undone at once if your provider refuses), create, edit, delete; Google's subtasks are shown under their task. The list opened first is the default one of your connector's preferences. The addresses in a task's notes are listed under them, ready to open. A long list scrolls inside its pane.
- **Contacts**: sorted by last name then first name, each shown as first name then last name; browse, search, filter by group, find the « other contacts » you met by mail (Google); every e-mail address, phone number and postal address of a contact is kept and edited one by one. **See the relationship** opens the Relations page on that person when the names match exactly. An « other contact » opens read-only, from what the search found: Google neither shows more of it nor changes it. The addresses in the notes open with a click.
- **Files**, on Google Drive or OneDrive: your drive and its folders, a search over all of it (names, or the content too), a file's preview, details and place, **Open in Drive** (or OneDrive), the trash with **Undo**. Create a folder, send files with the button or by dropping them on the list (up to the size the page states), rename, move through a walk of your folders, copy, share with someone or by a link anyone can follow, send a file by mail, keep a mail's attachment on the drive. A Google document downloads as PDF or Word, a sheet as Excel, a presentation as PDF or PowerPoint. The addresses in a file's description open with a click. What you use of your storage shows at the top of the drive.
- **Google Drive adds** its views (recent, starred, shared with you, shared drives), stars, and its trash: restored, deleted for good or emptied by hand, after a question. OneDrive has none of these to list; **Undo** after a trash works on a personal OneDrive, and SharePoint libraries are not listed. A copy on OneDrive finishes after it starts: the screen says it is under way, and it appears in its folder.
- **In the chat**, LIA does the same: it creates folders, renames, moves, copies, restores, and sends to your drive a file you attached or one it made. It shows a card before it shares a file, because the drive tells the person. Deleting for good stays yours alone.
- **Lights**: your rooms, lights and scenes; switch, dim and colour a lamp (only the controls it supports are shown), or a whole room at once. Rooms and lamps come in alphabetical order, and a lamp's temperature and colour wait in a section you open. A scene no room holds, a zone's, stays within reach under the rooms. A room or a lamp that is off recedes into the page, and lamps the bridge cannot reach stand in a group of their own, after those that answer. **All off** switches off what is lit, room by room; once nothing is lit, the same button says **All on**.

### Reading and changing a file

A file's detail and a message's attachments open in the page, on every device, the Android app included: a PDF whole, page after page, with its words selectable and a size you choose; a Word file laid out as its author wrote it; a Markdown formatted; a spreadsheet or a CSV as a table, a sheet at a time; an image; a text. A Google document comes through its PDF export, and with OneDrive a Word file or a slide deck comes as the PDF your drive renders. A slide deck attached to a mail or kept on Google Drive reads as text. **Enlarge** gives the document a window, a long text says it stops before its end, and a reading that fails says so and can be tried again. A link in a PDF is not followed: download the file for that. What is shown stops at the download ceiling the page states.

A plain text file (.txt, .md, .csv, .json…) up to the size the page allows can be changed here: **Edit the text**, then **Save**. If the file changed elsewhere meanwhile, nothing is written, your words stay on the screen and you can take the current version. A file that is not in UTF-8 opens for reading only. An Office file or a Google document is changed where it lives, behind **Open in Drive** (or OneDrive). LIA reads a text file, adds to it, and rewrites it when you ask: it shows you the whole new text on a card first, and a file changed since then is refused.

### What you exchanged with a contact

A contact's detail ends on your latest exchanges: the five latest messages they sent you, the five latest you sent them, the five latest meetings they organised with you invited, and the five latest you organised with them invited. A line opens in its tool. Mail and meetings stand side by side when the screen has the room. The calendar is read back over the last two years, in your default calendar, and the screen says so; a meeting someone else organised answers neither list, and one that names no organiser stands in a list of its own. If your mailbox or your calendar could not be read, it says so rather than showing nothing.

### Automatic replies and rules

With Gmail or Outlook, the mailbox settings hold your **vacation responder** and your **filters**, which Outlook calls rules, and LIA can read and change them too, after showing you a card. An Outlook reply has no subject, and a first day needs a last one. A rule moves mail to a folder, archives it or marks it read; a rule only Outlook can change stays listed without a way to delete it. An Outlook connection made before this feature asks to be reconnected once.

### Searching across your tools

Press **Ctrl+K** (⌘K on a Mac), or the magnifier beside the row of tools, type your words and press Enter: LIA looks through your mail, your calendars around today, your contacts and your files by name, all at once. Each tool shows its first results, and a result opens in its tool. A tool with nothing says so, and one that could not answer is named. With Outlook and iCloud the mail is searched in the inbox, which the results say. Tasks are not searched: no provider offers it.

### Zones, tone and scenes in motion

A **zone** sits after the rooms and works like one: its switch, its slider and its scenes. A room or a zone takes a **temperature** or a **colour** for all its lamps at once. A lamp your bridge cannot reach says so, and offers nothing until it answers. A scene whose palette can play has a button beside it that sets it **in motion**, and holds it still when pressed again. LIA reaches zones by name too, colours a whole room or zone, plays a scene in motion, and tells which lamps are not answering.

### Organising your tasks

**Today**, **Overdue** and **Coming up** gather what all your lists hold, beside the lists themselves. Rename a list, delete its completed tasks in one go, or press **Select** to complete, delete or move several tasks at once. A task can go to another list from its detail: with Microsoft To Do it is copied, then the original removed, and you are told if it ends up in both. With Microsoft To Do a task also has steps, an importance, a reminder and a repetition, and LIA writes them too. With Google Tasks you can move a task up or down its list.

### Repeating events

A new event can repeat: every day, week, month or year, until a date or for a number of times. Open an occurrence to change it alone, or choose **Edit the series** to change them all; deleting asks which you mean. With iCloud a change always reaches the whole series. LIA creates repeating events and reaches a whole series when you ask it to. A series keeps its first day: a new time applies to every occurrence.

### Organising your contacts

A contact holds a given and a family name, a job, a website, a birthday and a photo, whichever provider you use, and LIA writes them too. **Export** saves one contact or your whole address book as a vCard file; a file cut at its limit says so in its name. **Import** turns a vCard file into contacts and names any card it could not take. A group is made by adding someone to a name: type one in a contact's detail. Choose a group in the filter to rename or delete it; deleting a group never deletes its contacts. With Google Contacts, a contact met in mail can be saved among yours.

## Why can't I do something my provider's own app does?

Each screen offers only what your provider's connection lets LIA do — a gesture one provider lacks is not offered rather than offered and refused. A page cut at a limit always says so.

## What if I click twice, or the connection drops while sending?

The screen guards repeated submissions to reduce duplicate sends and creates. When the earlier submission is still recorded, a second click is told “already submitted” instead of sending again. If the answer was lost, the outcome is shown as unknown: check Sent or the relevant service before submitting again.
