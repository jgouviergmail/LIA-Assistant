# Tools — your services, by hand

## What is the « Tools » section?

**Tools** (in the header) opens your connected services directly — your mail, calendar, tasks, contacts, Drive files and Hue lights — so you can consult them and act on them **yourself**, the way you would in the provider's own application. No model is involved: it costs no token and nothing in euros, it answers as fast as your provider does, and it does exactly what you click.

A row of the six tools sits above the open one and switches between them in one tap — on a phone it scrolls sideways — and the section opens on the first tool that is ready. On a large screen, drag the line between two panes to widen one, or double-click it to give the pane its width back; this device remembers it. An item you open (a message, an event, a contact, a file) is part of the page's address, so the browser's back button closes it.

## Which tools appear, and why is one « To connect »?

A tool appears once its service is connected in **Settings › Connectors**: mail, calendar, contacts and tasks use the provider that is active for that category (Google, Microsoft or Apple — Apple has no tasks), files use Google Drive, and lights use your Hue bridge. A tool whose service is not connected yet is listed under **To connect**, with a link to the connectors. A tool your administrator switched off is not shown at all.

## Does LIA see or remember what I do in Tools?

What you do by hand is your act, not LIA's: it adds no entry to LIA's registers,
memory, habits, journal or AI usage costs. This does not switch off features you
enabled separately: the chat, routines, knowledge synchronisation and opted-in
monitoring retain their own access and permissions.

## What can I do with my mail?

Browse your folders (and your labels with Gmail), search, read a message as text or in its **original layout** — shown in an isolated frame where nothing can run, images and remote content blocked — mark messages read or unread, move them to the trash, apply labels (Gmail), write, reply, reply to all and forward (with your own files attached), keep and send drafts, and, with Gmail, set your vacation responder and filters. Attachments download as files, never opened inside the page. Typing a name in the recipients suggests your contacts' addresses.

## What can I do with my calendar?

See your calendars in **Agenda**, **Week** or **Month**, show or hide each calendar, create, change and delete events — all-day ones included, with a video call where your provider offers one, and telling the guests where your provider lets you choose. In a repeating series, a change or a deletion reaches **that occurrence only**, and the screen says so. Times are shown in your account's time zone. Typing a name in the guests suggests your contacts' addresses, as for a message's recipients.

## And my tasks, contacts, files and lights?

- **Tasks**: your lists and their tasks; tick a task done (it is undone at once if your provider refuses), create, edit, delete; Google's subtasks are shown under their task.
- **Contacts**: sorted by last name then first name, each shown as first name then last name; browse, search, filter by group (Google), find the « other contacts » you met by mail (Google); every e-mail address, phone number and postal address of a contact is kept and edited one by one. **See the relationship** opens the Relations page on that person when the names match exactly.
- **Files**: My Drive and its folders, a search over the whole Drive (names, or the content too), a file's details and where it lives, **Open in Drive**, the trash. A Google document downloads as PDF or Word, a sheet as Excel, a presentation as PDF or PowerPoint. Shared drives are not listed here.
- **Lights**: your rooms, lights and scenes; switch, dim and colour a lamp (only the controls it supports are shown), or a whole room at once. Rooms and lamps come in alphabetical order, and a lamp's temperature and colour wait in a section you open.

## Why can't I do something my provider's own app does?

Each screen offers only what your provider's connection lets LIA do — a gesture one provider lacks is not offered rather than offered and refused. A few gestures come later (archiving, moving, stars, answering an invitation, writing a repeating rule, uploading or renaming Drive files). A page cut at a limit always says so.

## What if I click twice, or the connection drops while sending?

The screen guards repeated submissions to reduce duplicate sends and creates. When the earlier submission is still recorded, a second click is told “already submitted” instead of sending again. If the answer was lost, the outcome is shown as unknown: check Sent or the relevant service before submitting again.
