# Personal radio

## How does the personal radio work?

Your radio is a station made **for you alone, while you listen**: LIA opens with your day, then brings in the news from the outlets you follow, translated into your language. A fixed running order decides what comes next; models write the words and the voices you chose speak them, over the station's own music.

**📻 Where:** the radio button in the header (on a phone, in the menu under the LIA logo), the radio card on your Home page, or the **Radio** page. Nothing is produced on a schedule, and nothing is kept for replay.

**🔎 Every sentence has its source:** **Transcript and sources** shows who speaks, what is said and the outlet or the part of your day each line rests on. Advertising and sales pitches are left out.

**⚙️ Tune it** in **Settings › Radio** (Preferences tab, Voice & Media): programmes, your life on air, news sources, voices, listening and spending. If your instance does not offer the radio, its page says so.

## How do I start, pause and stop the radio, and when does it stop by itself?

**▶️ Start:** press **Start the radio** in the header, on the Home card or on the Radio page. The Radio page also sets this session's **Automatic stop** (as in your settings, after a number of minutes, or none) and **Listening in company**. The station's music starts right away and the bar tells you when the first voice should arrive.

**⏯️ While it plays:** a bar under the header follows you on every page — the station, what is on air, the minutes left, the cost so far, **Pause**, **Stop** and **Transcript and sources**. Your keyboard's or headset's play/pause key works too. Starting the radio again, even on another device, replaces the session under way.

**⏹️ It stops by itself** when:
• the automatic stop arrives — it counts listening time, so a pause pushes it back, and the station signs off with a short farewell
• nobody is listening: the page was closed, or the pause lasted too long
• its programmes repeatedly cannot be produced
• a spending limit is reached

The bar then says why and offers **Start again**.

## Which programmes does the radio broadcast, and how often?

In **Settings › Radio › Programmes**, you set how often each programme comes back — **Never**, **Rarely**, **Normally** or **Often**:
• **Personal journal**: your day in three editions — what's planned in the morning, what you did and what's left at midday, a look back and the days ahead in the evening
• **Headlines**, **News bulletin**, **Brief** and **The figure**: always stories you haven't heard yet
• **Analysis** and **In depth** with an expert, the station's **Editorial**, **The debate** and **Talking it through** between its commentators: one story, from an angle — they may come back to a story you heard, never to the one the previous programme just told

The **Opening** and the **Sign-off** belong to the station. When LIA sends you a notification while you listen, a **News flash** breaks in, then the programme resumes where it stopped — unless you listen in company or switched off **Notifications** in your life on air.

## What can the radio say about my own day?

In **Settings › Radio › Your life on air**, each source has its own switch, and a source switched off is **never read**, not merely left unsaid: appointments, reminders, tasks, commitments, workboard tickets, birthdays, unread and sent e-mails, what LIA did for you, the weather, health, meetings, LIA's notifications, people to get back to, knowledge spaces, kept answers and the conversation under way. Each switch says what it reads.

Your day airs in the **Personal journal**: set that programme to **Never** and nothing personal is read at all. With **Listening in company** — for one session on the Radio page, or by default under **Listening** — nothing about you airs: only the weather is read, for the opening.

What the radio opened appears in the **Consultations** tab of your **Registers**, like any other reading. Your radio settings and sites are exported and deleted with your account.

## Where does the radio's news come from, and can I add my own sites?

A shared newsroom reads public feeds for the whole instance, and only while someone uses the radio. In **Settings › Radio › News sources**:
• **The radio's sources**: major outlets and independent voices in several languages, all ticked by default — everything airs translated into your language. Untick what you don't want to hear. Each shows how many stories it published recently, how many you haven't heard, and **Not answering** when its last readings failed.
• **Your sites**: type an address, press **Check** — the radio finds the site's feed and describes it — then **Add**. A site can be paused (kept, no longer read), renamed (the name heard on air) or removed, up to a number your administrator sets. A site that refuses robots or publishes no feed is refused, with the reason.

Advertising, sponsored content, deals and purchase pitches are left out; genuine invoices, reservations and editorial, scientific or economic reporting stay eligible. When an outlet offers no readable article, the station uses only its title or summary and never pretends to have read more.

## Can the radio bring me news about my interests?

Yes, if you have your own **Brave Search** or **Perplexity** key in your connectors. When a session starts, your strongest interests — as many as your administrator allows — are searched with **your key** (Brave first, Perplexity otherwise), and the stories found can go on air. The news programmes then take turns between your sources and what the search found.
• The search runs on your account with that provider, so LIA does not record that spend; each search appears in your **Consultations** register.
• An interest searched recently is not searched again at every start.
• With **Listening in company**, or when your administrator has switched interests off, none of your interests is read, searched or mentioned.

Your interests and remembered preferences also help the news programmes pick their stories — as context, never stated on air as facts about you.

## How do I name my station and choose its personality and voices?

In **Settings › Radio › Voices**:
• **Station name**: said on air by the host and shown on the player; leave it empty for "LIA Radio" in your language. A session keeps the name it started with.
• **Station personality**: **The same as in the chat**, or another of LIA's personalities.
• A voice for each role — **LIA**, the **Anchor**, the **Expert**, the **Editorialist** — and for the three **Debate and discussion commentators**, **Automatic** by default. A commentator never shares a voice with LIA, the anchor or another commentator: those voices are greyed out.

Choosing a voice plays a short sample in your language; a radio that is playing pauses so you can hear it (a meeting recording or a Live session keeps priority). The voices are those of the engine your administrator set for the radio, shown by default in your language and multilingual ones; your choices are kept when the administrator changes model at the same provider. On engines that voice several speakers, a debate is recorded in takes, never with more voices per take than the engine accepts. In a discussion, a debate, the editorial, the opening or the sign-off, a voice may open its line with a chuckle, a laugh or a sigh when it can perform them — twice per programme at most by default (a bound your administrator sets), never during the news.

## What does checking by a second model do, and why is it off by default?

Every programme already passes an automatic editor, at no extra cost: each line must cite the facts it rests on, a line stating a figure no cited source gives is removed, and a programme that loses too much is not aired.

**Checking by a second model** (**Settings › Radio › Listening**) goes further: before a programme airs, another model compares its sourced lines with the facts they cite, removes the unsupported ones, and may drop the programme if too little remains or the check fails.
• **No check** · **Check the news** · **Check every programme**
• the settings say how many kinds of programme your choice has re-read

It is **off by default** because every reading is billed and can delay the listening; it stays one choice away. An administrator can set another default for accounts that never chose — the settings always show the level that applies to you.

## Can I read the articles the radio talks about?

Yes. On the **Radio** page (or **Transcript and sources** on the bar), the programme on air is shown line by line — the line being spoken is highlighted — with the outlet and date each sentence rests on, and what comes next.

Below, **Articles of this session** lists every story cited, in the order it aired. Open one to read it whole, **in your language**:
• it is translated when you open it, **billed once**, then read again at no cost — its cost is shown
• **Original** opens it on the outlet's site in a new tab, with no translation asked
• when the outlet shares only a summary, or the article is long, the page says so and points to the outlet
• past your radio's spending limit, it is shown in its original language, untranslated

The list stays once the session is over, so you can finish what you opened, until you start the radio again.

## What does the radio cost, and how is it limited?

Each programme is written and voiced for you, so it is billed to your account and counts in your usage like any exchange: the writing, the speech, the optional model checks, the translations of the articles you open and the voice samples. The shared newsroom costs you nothing, and searches made with your own Brave or Perplexity key are paid to that provider, not recorded by LIA.

**💶 In view while you listen:** the bar shows the **cost so far** and, once enough has been produced, an estimate for the listening you planned — or per hour, with no automatic stop.

**🛑 Limits:** your account's usage limits apply, and your administrator may also give the radio its own budget over a rolling 24 hours, shown in **Settings › Radio › Your radio's spending**. Past it, a start is refused, a session stops before its next programme and articles stay untranslated — and the radio tells you when it can play again.

## Why does the radio sometimes play only music, or never repeat a story?

The station's music plays throughout, from the start to the farewell, in a mood that suits the hour or the news, and steps well back under every voice. You hear it alone:
• while the first programme is prepared — the opening is made first, from the time and the weather only, so the first voice comes quickly
• for a few seconds between two programmes, and while the next one is being written
• when a programme had nothing to say, or the editor refused its script: it does not air and the station moves on (a debate the voice engine cannot cast with distinct voices is not planned either)

**🔁 No repeats:** a story you heard is not told again, even by another outlet or in other words — only the angle programmes may come back to it. When everything has been heard, the station says so once (**Nothing new**). To hear everything again, use **Forget what I heard** in **Settings › Radio › News sources**: it asks first and cannot be undone.

## Is my radio private, and is the news text trusted?

Your programme is made for your account alone and is never shared with another account; the shared newsroom only collects public pages. The text of a news item is treated as data, never as instructions, and a script whose lines do not rest on usable sources is refused rather than aired. The player shows the sources and the cost instead of presenting the station's words as an uncited fact.

## Does a speaking avatar accompany the radio?

No. The radio is audio only: the speaking video avatar does not accompany it.
