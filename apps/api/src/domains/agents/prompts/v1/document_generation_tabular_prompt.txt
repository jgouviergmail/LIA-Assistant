FORMAT FAMILY: TABULAR OUTPUT (Spreadsheets & CSV)
- Column Design: Each column represents ONE atomic attribute with uniform type and unit. Headers must be unique, non-empty and self-explanatory; a unit belongs in the header or the column's `unit`, never inside a data cell.
- Strict Parsing Hygiene: Every written cell must allow automated column typing:
  * Numbers: Plain digits with a dot decimal separator (e.g. `1234.5`, never commas or spaces).
  * Dates: ISO 8601 (`YYYY-MM-DD`, or `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`).
  * Percentages: Number followed by `%` (e.g. `12.5%`).
  * Empty cells: Represented as empty strings `""`.
  * Never write a formula or a computed result in a cell: declare it (Computed Columns).
- Column Semantics (`columns`): declare what a column IS when its cells alone cannot say it — its `kind` (`amount`, `quantity`, `percent`, `date`, `datetime`, `year`, `code`, `text`) and its `unit` (a currency code such as `EUR`, or a unit symbol such as `kg`). The renderer formats it in the reader's conventions (digit grouping, currency side, date order). Declare every amount and quantity; declare `year` and `code` columns so they are never grouped like numbers.
- Computed Columns: never compute a value yourself. Declare a column with a `formula` and leave its cells empty: the renderer writes a live spreadsheet formula and computes every value exactly.
  * Grammar: names of columns of the SAME sheet, and of `inputs`, in braces; numbers with a dot; `+ - * / ^` and parentheses; `ROUND(x, n)`, `ABS(x)`, `MIN(a, b)`, `MAX(a, b)`; `SUM({{Column}})` and `AVERAGE({{Column}})` over a whole column; `RUNNING_SUM({{Column}})` for the total from the first row down to this one; `PREVIOUS({{Column}})` for the row above (the first row has none: its value stays empty).
  * Conditions: `IF(condition, value if true, value if false)`, both values numbers. A condition compares two numbers (`=`, `<>`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`), or a text column with a text in double quotes (`=` or `<>` only, whatever the case), and combines with `AND(…)`, `OR(…)`, `NOT(…)`. A condition never compares a value that may be missing — a column or key figure that divides, averages or reads the row above, or reads one that does: compare the quantities themselves, `{{Balance}} >= 0.2 * {{Income}}` rather than `{{Savings rate}} >= 0.2`. In a formula a percentage is a fraction: 15% is `0.15`.
  * Nothing else: no cell address, no text as a value, and a column never reads itself — in its own formula its name is the input of that name: a `Unit price` column showing the `Unit price` input on every row is `{{Unit price}}`. A value chosen among many categories is written in its cells, or read from a column of its own.
  * Every column holds its values: written in its cells, or computed by its formula. A column left empty stays empty in the file and reads as 0 in every formula: leave one empty only for the reader to fill.
  * Examples: margin `{{Revenue}} - {{Cost}}`; margin rate `({{Revenue}} - {{Cost}}) / {{Revenue}}` with `kind` `percent`; share of the total `{{Sales}} / SUM({{Sales}})`; growth `{{Sales}} / PREVIOUS({{Sales}}) - 1`; cumulative sales `RUNNING_SUM({{Sales}})`; volume discount `IF({{Units}} >= 100, {{Price}} * 0.9, {{Price}})`; amount already paid `IF({{Status}} = "Paid", {{Amount}}, 0)`; duration in days `{{End}} - {{Start}}`; price with tax `{{Price}} * (1 + {{VAT rate}})`.
  * At most {formula_max_chars} characters per formula. A formula the renderer cannot compute is dropped: the column keeps the cells you wrote, or goes when you wrote none, with every formula reading it.
- Totals (`total`): ask for a totals row per column (`sum`, `average`, `min`, `max`, `count`) instead of writing a total row yourself.
- Inputs (`inputs`): put every assumption a computation depends on (a rate, a unit price, a growth hypothesis) in `inputs` — a name, a value, its `kind` and a `note` saying where it comes from. Formulas read inputs by name, and the reader can change them.
- Summaries (`summary`): to sum, average or count a sheet by category, declare a `summary` (`by` a column of words or whole numbers, its `measures`, its `function`) instead of writing a second table: the renderer computes it from the data, live. A column's formula reads its own sheet and the inputs only: a quantity drawn from several sheets or rows is a key figure, never a table of empty cells.
- Key Figures (`figures`): the quantities the workbook exists to answer — a year's income, the share left after expenses — each a `label` and a `formula`, with its `kind` and `unit` declared as a column's, at most {workbook_figures_max}. A key figure has no row: its formula reads the `inputs` and the other key figures by name, and a sheet's whole column as `SUM({{Sheet/Column}})` or `AVERAGE({{Sheet/Column}})`, with the operators, functions and `IF` of a column's grammar. The renderer writes each one live, on a sheet of their own after the inputs. Examples: annual income `SUM({{Monthly budget/Income}}) + {{Bonus}}`; savings rate `{{Annual balance}} / {{Annual income}}` with `kind` `percent`.
- Charts (`chart`): a sheet's chart is drawn natively beside it and linked to its cells, so it follows every change; a computed column can be drawn.
- Drop-down Lists (`choices`): for a column that only admits a few values (a status, a priority), list them, at most {column_choices_max}.
- Emphasis (`better`): for a key measure, say whether `higher` or `lower` is better; the renderer colours its values accordingly.
- Sorting & Grouping: Sort records homogeneously (chronological, alphabetical, or priority order). Split fundamentally distinct entities into separate named sheets rather than combining incompatible schemas.
- Discernment: declare what serves the reader. A plain list needs no formula; a financial or operational table needs its totals, its rates and its assumptions.
- CSV: only the first sheet is written, as data for another program: computed columns carry their values; inputs, totals, summaries, key figures and charts are not written.
