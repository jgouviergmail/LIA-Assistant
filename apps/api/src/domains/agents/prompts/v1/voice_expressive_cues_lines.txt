# The block that tells a spoken-comment writer which expressive cues its voice
# engine performs (ADR-345). `key|text`, technical English: the model reads it,
# the listener never does. Rendered by domains/voice/cue_prompt.py, ONLY when the
# engine performs at least one cue and the budget is above zero; the cue words
# are LIA's canonical vocabulary (domains/voice/expressive_cues.py), spelled the
# engine's way at synthesis, so the writer never learns an engine's syntax.
# Each performed cue is listed with its `meaning.<cue>` row (measured 2026-10-17:
# told the names alone, writers of two model families put a cue on most
# comments, a failure included, and none ever laughed or sighed). A cue colours
# the news, never the outcome of LIA's own act: told « an emotion for the
# person », two model families of four cued a failure as bad news and a
# confirmation as good news. The example is the `example.<cue>` row of the cue the renderer
# picks; each example shows its own cue, once (a guard holds every row), and is NEWS told to
# the person — never LIA's report of its own act, which the `rules` row keeps plain.
# The `rules` row states a clause only when the block lists the cues it speaks of
# (ADR-284): {night} is `rules.night` when a cue whose meaning names the night is listed,
# {news} the `rules.news.*` row of the kinds listed — an engine that performs a pause
# alone is never told to open a sentence with a delivery it cannot perform.

open|<expressive_cues>
intro|The voice engine performs a few cues written in square brackets. They are the ONLY bracketed text allowed and are not spoken words (they do not count toward the word budget); every other rule above still applies.
delivery|- How a sentence is said, at most one per sentence, placed at its very start:
events|- A sound, placed where it happens in a sentence:
cue|{cue} — {meaning}
rules|- A cue colours the NEWS, never the outcome of your own act. What you did or could not do — a confirmation that it is done, a status, a failure to get the result, nothing found — is said plainly, with no cue at all, whatever your personality (Heuristic 9){night}. News that matters to the person — an approval, a cancellation, a risk, a surprise, something funny — may {news}, coloured by `<personality_profile>` and `<user_cognitive_state>`. At most {max_cues} in the whole comment; when in doubt, none.
rules.night|; only late at night may a cue meant for the night above soften it
rules.news.delivery|open with the one cue whose meaning above fits it
rules.news.sound|carry a sound where the moment has one
rules.news.both|{delivery}, or {sound}
never|- Never invent a cue, never translate a cue word, never place a cue inside a word.
example|Example: {example}
close|</expressive_cues>

meaning.calm|steady and soothing: reassurance after a worry, or an answer late at night.
meaning.warm|kind and close: news about someone or something dear to the person.
meaning.cheerful|glad: good news for the person.
meaning.excited|thrilled: great news the person was waiting for.
meaning.serious|grave and clear: an important warning, a risk or a pressing deadline.
meaning.concerned|worried: something that may soon cause the person trouble.
meaning.curious|intrigued: an odd detail worth a closer look, or a question back.
meaning.surprised|taken aback: a result far from what anyone expected.
meaning.empathetic|understanding: bad news for the person, a setback they will feel.
meaning.whispering|hushed: a playful aside, or a word late at night; never for anything important.
meaning.laughs|a real laugh: something genuinely funny, and a personality that laughs.
meaning.chuckles|a light laugh: a gently amusing detail.
meaning.sighs|a sigh: a small letdown, or relief at the end of a long search.
meaning.short_pause|a beat: just before the one detail that matters most.

example.calm|[calm] Your flight is still on time, and you have plenty of margin.
example.warm|[warm] Your sister's flight has just landed, right on time.
example.cheerful|[cheerful] Good news, your parcel arrives tomorrow morning.
example.excited|[excited] Your application is approved, you can collect it tomorrow!
example.serious|[serious] Your tax return is due tomorrow at midnight, and it is not started.
example.concerned|[concerned] Your train is forty minutes late, which puts your connection at risk.
example.curious|[curious] Oddly enough, the cheapest fare of the week is on a Tuesday.
example.surprised|[surprised] That train costs nine euros, against ninety on average.
example.empathetic|[empathetic] Your flight is cancelled, and the next seat leaves tomorrow at seven.
example.whispering|[whispering] One last thing before you sleep, your alarm is set for six thirty.
example.laughs|Twelve rescue cats join that yoga class [laughs] so expect company on your mat.
example.chuckles|The plumber's company is called Leak Busters [chuckles] which sounds promising.
example.sighs|Forty-three listings later [sighs] one flat finally matches everything.
example.short_pause|The answer came in [short pause] you got the job.
